About

Simulation, ML and physics for discovery

I am a Ph.D. candidate in Materials Science & Engineering at the University of Maryland, College Park, advised by Prof. Yifei Mo, expecting to graduate in Spring 2027.

I specialize in high-throughput screening, where I use machine-learning interatomic potentials (MLIPs) to search thousands of compounds quickly and DFT and AIMD to validate the most promising ones. In my work in Advanced Materials, I devised Δd, a simple, transferable descriptor that classifies ion-migration pathways as low- or high-barrier directly from the crystal structure, and used it to screen Li-, Na-, K- and Mg-ion conductors.

My broader experience includes training and fine-tuning MLIPs, building MLIP-based computational tools, and agentic AI for materials research.

Research focus

What I work on

High-throughput screening with ML & DFT

Promising materials are rare in vast chemical spaces, so discovery demands systematic search. MLIPs approach DFT accuracy at orders-of-magnitude lower cost, with DFT and AIMD reserved for high-fidelity validation of top candidates.

Discovery through underlying physics

Physics-based descriptors link simple material features to properties at negligible cost, bypassing expensive first-principles calculations for database-scale prescreening while retaining mechanistic interpretability that guides materials design (e.g., Δd).

Agentic AI & autonomous workflows

Automated, fault-tolerant pipelines ensure reproducibility and provenance at scale. LLM agents close the loop from hypothesis to validation, a step toward self-driving laboratories.

A Simple Descriptor for Solid-State Ionic Conductors Across Chemistries

R.S. Saravanan, C.E. Schwarz, F. Adams, K. Amirian, Y. Mo · Advanced Materials (2026)

Descriptors like the Goldschmidt tolerance factor and the d-band center reduce complex physics to one intuitive number that guides materials design. Ionic conductors lacked such a descriptor: existing ones capture the migration physics of one chemistry but fail to transfer to others. To find one that does, we looked at what sets the barrier itself, and found it is largely determined by how much closer the moving ion gets to its neighbors at the transition state than at its equilibrium sites. Because this distance contraction, Δd, is measured relative to the ion's own equilibrium environment, it transfers across chemistries.

  • With no fitted parameters, Δd separates low- from high-barrier pathways for Li, Na, K and Mg (AUC–PR ≈ 80).
  • A single 0.65 Å cutoff screens the ICSD, recovers most known fast-ion conductors and finds new candidates, several predicted above 1 mS/cm by AIMD.
  • Explains why Na-ion conductors are scarcer than Li-ion ones, and why framework distortion, bottleneck opening and polyanion rotation all lower barriers.
  • Identifies migration pathways from the crystal structure alone, without NEB, making it a fast prescreening tool.
